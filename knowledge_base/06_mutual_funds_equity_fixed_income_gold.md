# Mutual Funds, Equity, Fixed Income and Gold

## Mutual funds
A mutual fund pools investor money and invests according to its scheme documents. Explain NAV, expense ratio, exit load, riskometer, direct/regular plans, growth/IDCW options and taxation only using current official documents.

- Approximate units purchased = investment amount ÷ applicable NAV, subject to transaction rules.
- Expense ratio reduces scheme assets and affects investor outcomes over time.
- Exit load and tax treatment depend on the scheme and current rules.

## Equity
Equity ownership can provide growth but prices can fall substantially. Avoid describing equity as suitable for every person or every time horizon.

## Fixed income
Risks include default/credit risk, duration and interest-rate risk, reinvestment risk, inflation risk and liquidity risk. A higher yield may reflect higher risk.

Bond price and yield generally move in opposite directions, but the effect depends on maturity, duration, credit spread and cash flows.

## Gold
Gold can diversify some risks but has price volatility, storage/product costs and no guaranteed income. Compare physical gold, ETFs, funds and digital products using current product documents and regulatory status.

## Dynamic verification
Before discussing a specific fund, ETF, bond or platform, check current SEBI documents, scheme information document, factsheet, riskometer, expense ratio, exit load, taxation and intermediary registration.
