# Budgeting, Cash Flow and Saving

## Core concepts
Budgeting is a forward-looking plan for income, essential spending, discretionary spending, debt payments, saving and investing. A budget is not a legal rule and must reflect irregular income, dependants, health needs and local costs.

## Useful calculations
- Net cash flow = total inflows − total outflows.
- Savings = after-tax income − expenses − debt payments.
- Savings rate = savings ÷ after-tax income × 100.
- Expense ratio = category expense ÷ total expenses × 100.
- Emergency-fund months = liquid emergency savings ÷ essential monthly expenses.
- Real return approximation = nominal return − inflation rate. More exact: `(1 + nominal return)/(1 + inflation) − 1`.

## Budget frameworks
The 50/30/20 framework is a flexible starting heuristic, not a universal prescription:
- needs
- wants
- savings/debt reduction
Adjust it for rent, family responsibilities, medical costs, variable income and high-interest debt.

## Emergency fund
Estimate essential expenses, not lifestyle expenses. Keep the reserve in accessible, low-risk instruments appropriate to the user's situation. Separate emergency savings from long-term investments and market-linked assets.

Do not state that every person must hold exactly 3 or 6 months. Explain that the appropriate amount depends on income stability, dependants, insurance, debt, job risk and access to support.

## Sinking funds
A sinking fund is a planned reserve for a known future expense.
Formula: monthly contribution = (target amount − current reserve) ÷ months remaining.
Include inflation and uncertainty for long-term goals.

## Dynamic cautions
Do not recommend a fixed SIP amount or fixed savings percentage without asking about income, expenses, debt, emergency savings and goals.
